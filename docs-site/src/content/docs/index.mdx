A notebook of notes to my future self. Mostly about signal processing, hardware, and the systems that sit underneath the software I write. Incomplete on purpose.

## What this is

I keep running into the same five or six ideas in different disguises — sampling, feedback, locality, entropy, the cost of abstraction — and I'd rather write them down once, clearly, than reconstruct them from memory every time. So here they are, in public, in varying states of polish.

Some pages are rehearsed. Some are half-formed. The tag `draft` means I wouldn't yet trust it in an interview; `wip` means there's a graph I haven't drawn.

<Aside type="tip" title="A small promise">
Nothing here is written to impress. If a sentence reads like marketing, that's a bug — please [let me know](mailto:hi@josueaparcedo.dev).
</Aside>

## How it's laid out

The sidebar is organized by topic, not chronology. Within a topic, pages try to move from gentle to gnarly. If a page assumes something, it will say so in the first paragraph — look for the *"this assumes"* note.

- **Signals** — transforms, filters, the parts of DSP that felt like magic until I slowed down.
- **Hardware** — build logs with schematics and failure modes.
- **Systems** — mental models for memory, concurrency, and runtimes.
- **Off-topic** — film, books, and other careful obsessions.

## How it's built

The site runs on [Astro](https://astro.build) with [Starlight](https://starlight.astro.build) for these pages, and a small MDX pipeline so writing stays close to plain text. Search is `pagefind`, built at deploy time; every page is also plain HTML for anyone running with JS off.

The stack here is a reaction to writing for years in heavier systems. I wanted something I could open, read in under a minute, and close — both the page and the codebase.

## Cadence

I try to ship one finished page a month and keep two or three drafts in flight. If a draft sits for more than `90` days without movement, I delete it — the idea either comes back or it didn't matter.
